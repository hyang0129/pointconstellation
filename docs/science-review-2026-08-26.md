# Point Constellation — Senior-author decision memo

## 1. State of the science

The project has one robust, replicated finding and two weaker ones. Robust: at a fixed, byte-exact 50-byte stream (ModelNet40, N=2048, K=8, q=12, 0.1953 bpp), a K×3 coordinate-only constellation chosen by recurrent refinement with frozen-decoder gradient feedback beats deterministic FPS through the same hash-checked decoder on official MPEG `pc_error` metrics, in 6/6 independently trained decoders, on both the validation split and 8 held-out categories. Weaker: (i) coordinates beat a byte-matched *internal* feature latent by ~30%, but under an unequal training protocol (the like-for-like Exp 018 comparison failed); (ii) the constellation sits below G-PCC's fixed-syntax floor in a 0.19–0.25 bpp corridor, but G-PCC dominates from ~0.27 bpp and the corridor is partly a header artifact. The mechanism is ~80% encoder-side gradient search through a frozen decoder (Exp 005: 22.9% with gradient vs 5.1% without; Exp 013 no-feedback CI [-0.32, 8.68]), and a plain 16-eval Adam-STE search beats the learned refiner by 6.3%/9.7% on a 16-cloud probe. All three reviewers score 3.5–4 and converge on the same verdict: not a codec paper; reframe as representation + decoder-aware simplification.

## 2. What is established vs. not

**Established**
- Decoder-aware constellation refinement beats FPS through the same frozen decoder at 50 B: 6/6 decoders, official D1/D2, CIs exclude zero on val and cat-OOD (Exp 020), replicated on procedural data with 3 seeds (Exp 013).
- The gain is real geometry, not sample completion, at least on meshes: fresh-resample Chamfer within 0.5 pts of source Chamfer (Exp 019).
- Rate accounting is byte-exact (14 B header + ceil(3Kq/8)); every reconstruction is decoded from a serialized stream; feature codec is exactly byte-matched.
- The mechanism is dominated by frozen-decoder gradient feedback; learned recurrent/competitive allocation adds ~4–5% (CI touches zero).
- Learned hard/soft selectors (Exp 002–004) lose to FPS by 28–43%; this negative result is solid and useful.
- Decoder seed explains 99.998% of baseline variance; 4 epochs + EMA stabilizes it.

**Not established**
- Codec competitiveness: one stabilized rate point, no entropy coding, no variable K, no BD-rate, G-PCC dominates ≥0.27 bpp, no overlapping-rate published codec with ≥3 points.
- "Coordinates > feature latent": confounded (feature codec 2 epochs/no EMA vs 4 epochs + EMA); Exp 018 like-for-like was −6.97% [−29.23, 10.78].
- "Beats point selection": only deterministic FPS tested at the headline point; no k-means, Poisson-disk, best-of-N random, SampleNet/APES.
- "Semi-amortized inference is the contribution": refiner loses to 16-eval Adam-STE search.
- Generalization beyond a 672-mesh ModelNet40 subset; "validation" is the official test split and was reused for decisions in Exp 016–020.
- Gate B on procedural data (analytic surface distance, independent resampling, bin perturbation): never run.

## 3. Consensus fatal gaps (raised by all three reviewers unless noted)

1. **Refiner loses to Adam-STE search; Gate E unrun.** Close: full-scale multi-start Adam-STE (starts: FPS, k-means, random; budgets 16/64/256 evals) on 128 val + 32 OOD × 6 decoders, official D1/D2. Reframe the refiner as amortization: report an encode-time-vs-D1 Pareto curve (refiner 40 ms vs Adam at each budget). If refiner is not on the Pareto front at any budget, drop the inference claim and keep it as an ablation.
2. **Baseline set is FPS-only.** Close: k-means/Lloyd (K=8), weighted k-means, Poisson-disk, random-start FPS, best-of-N random, one learned sampler (SampleNet or APES) trained through the frozen decoder; all at 50 B through identical serialization.

## 4. Recommended framing

**Choose: representation + learned (decoder-aware) simplification.** Justification: it is the only framing whose headline is CI-backed on official metrics across 6/6 decoders; it does not require winning a codec race the evidence loses above 0.27 bpp; and it subsumes the inference result as a supporting amortization trade-off rather than a contested novelty. The codec framing is dead on one rate point; the amortized-inference framing is defeated by the Adam probe and by the neural-image-compression encoder-side-optimization literature.

**Title:** *Coordinates as the Code: Ultra-Low-Rate Shape Bottlenecks from Decoder-Aware Point Constellations*

**Abstract (3 sentences):** We show that an unordered, quantized K×3 coordinate set with no feature channels, decoded by a shared frozen geometric decoder, is a viable tens-of-bytes shape bottleneck — but only when the constellation is chosen by optimization against the decoder rather than by geometric point selection. At an exact 50-byte stream on ModelNet40, decoder-aware refinement improves official MPEG D1/D2 over farthest-point sampling by 16.5%/34.7% through the same decoder (6 independent decoders, category-OOD transfer), and matches or beats byte-matched learned feature latents under identical training. A recurrent refiner amortizes per-cloud gradient search at a fraction of the encode cost, and we position the representation on the rate-distortion map against G-PCC and learned codecs, showing where it helps (below ~0.25 bpp) and where it does not.

# Appendix A: adversarial reviews

## Reviewer: novelty — score 4

Reject as currently framed (borderline weak-reject). The engineering discipline is unusually good (real 50-byte bitstreams, hash-checked frozen decoders, official pc_error, paired hierarchical bootstraps, predeclared gates), but the paper as it exists today has no defensible headline. The codec framing collapses to one stabilized rate point (K=8, 50 B) in a 0.19-0.25 bpp corridor that G-PCC's ~40-byte fixed parameter-set syntax partly manufactures, G-PCC dominates from 0.27 bpp upward, and the only overlapping published-codec point (pcc_geo_cnn_v2 q6 at 20k steps, cluster-only, not yet in the docs) is a self-adapted low-rate protocol of a 2019 codec that the authors themselves flagged as undertrained at 5k. The inference framing is well replicated (6/6 decoders, D1 +16.5%, D2 +34.7% over FPS) but its novelty is thin: the gain is ~80% attributable to encoder-side optimization through a frozen decoder (Exp 005: 22.9% with gradient vs 5.1% without), which is exactly the Campos/Yang-2020/van Rozendaal/Guo per-instance latent refinement line, and a plain 16-step Adam-STE search using identical information already beats the learned refiner by 6-10%. The representation framing (coordinates beat a feature latent at matched bytes, +29.9%) is the most interesting positioning but rests on a home-grown PointNet feature codec trained under a weaker protocol (2 epochs, no EMA/selection) than the constellation (4 epochs + EMA + calibration), and the pre-stabilization version of the same comparison (Exp 018) failed. Add to that a 672-mesh ModelNet40 subset with the official test split reused as 'validation' across five iterative experiments, no learned-sampling baselines (SampleNet/APES) despite the 'beats point selection' claim, no k-means/Lloyd free-coordinate baseline, no learned-octree or FPS+Draco corridor competitors, and a self-admitted outstanding literature review. Reviewers in this area will identify the FPS-only baseline and the Adam-beats-refiner result immediately.

**Best framing:** A representation paper, not a codec paper: 'Coordinates as the code: a quantized, unordered K x 3 point constellation with no feature channels is a better ultra-low-rate (~50 byte) shape bottleneck than a byte-matched learned feature latent, provided the constellation is chosen by decoder-aware optimization rather than geometric point selection.' Lead with the frozen-decoder evidence (6/6 decoders, official D1 +16.5% / D2 +34.7% over FPS, coordinate-only > feature latent at matched bytes and matched training protocol), present the recurrent refiner as an amortized approximation to per-cloud gradient search with an explicit encode-cost/quality trade-off (and cite the neural-image-compression encoder-side optimization line as the mechanism's origin), and relegate G-PCC/pcc_geo_cnn_v2 to a header-normalised positioning figure that shows where the representation sits on the RD map without claiming codec competitiveness. This is honest, matches what the CIs support, and is a credible workshop-to-main-track contribution once the k-means/Adam/learned-sampler baselines and the equal-protocol feature-codec comparison are in.

**Strengths**
- Rigorous rate accounting: every reported byte is a real serialized fixed-width stream (14-byte header with K/precision/N/mode + ceil(3Kq/8)), decoded from the stream before evaluation; feature-codec baseline is exactly byte-matched at 50 B.
- Strong replication hygiene for the FPS comparison: 6 independently trained decoders x 3 refiner seeds, hash-checked frozen decoders, paired hierarchical (category/cloud/decoder) bootstrap, official MPEG pc_error D1/D2 on a 12-bit grid with recorded binary SHA; 6/6 decoder marginals positive, val D1 +16.52% (11.43-21.79), D2 +34.65% (27.07-40.81), OOD similar.
- Honest negative-result trail: learned hard selectors lose to FPS by 28-43% (Exp 002-004), soft selectors collapse, no-gradient refiner ablation isolates the mechanism (5% vs 23%), Exp 018 feature-codec gate failure is reported rather than hidden.
- Clean anti-steganography representation contract (quantization in loop, half-bin jitter, surface-proximity loss, no per-point features) and fresh-resampling checks on ModelNet40 showing gains within 0.5 pts of exact-sample gains.
- Category-OOD split (8 unseen ModelNet40 classes) with gains of the same magnitude as in-distribution, and the same decoder trained only on FPS messages, which if anything favors the baseline.
- Decoder-seed variance decomposition (99.998% of baseline variance) and a stabilization protocol that produced a clean, reproducible operating point; the G-PCC crossover analysis is candid (dominated by K=16).
- Self-aware documentation: README disclaims compression competitiveness, landscape doc calls novelty 'a hypothesis, not a claim', and the sprint doc predeclares gates and stop conditions.

**Fatal/major**
- No defensible headline claim. Codec claim: one stabilized rate point (50 B), G-PCC wins from ~0.27 bpp up (2.3x RMSE at 0.45-0.62 bpp), no BD-rate, no entropy coding or variable K on the constellation side; the docs themselves say compression competitiveness is not claimed. A codec-titled paper with a single point and a 0.19-0.25 bpp corridor will be rejected outright.
- The win corridor vs G-PCC is confounded by fixed syntax overhead: ~40 of G-PCC's 46-62 corridor bytes are SPS/GPS/slice headers vs a 14-byte constellation header. Payload-only, G-PCC spends ~21 B for 53 points (Chamfer 0.134) vs 36 B for 8 points (0.109). No header-normalised or sequence-amortised comparison exists; a reviewer will argue the corridor is an artifact of comparing against an unstripped standards bitstream at descriptor scale. Learned octree entropy models at depth 4-6 (OctAttention/VoxelContext-Net) or FPS/SampleNet-to-K + Draco would likely erase it and were not run.
- Thin novelty of the inference mechanism relative to encoder-side RD optimization in neural image compression (Campos et al. content-adaptive optimization, Yang et al. 2020 improving inference, van Rozendaal et al. per-instance fine-tuning, Guo et al. iterative refinement, Djelouah & Schroers). Exp 005 attributes ~18 of 23 points to frozen-decoder gradient feedback; the learned recurrent/competitive component adds ~4-5% with a validation CI that includes zero in Exp 013 (-0.32, 8.68). The paper would be presenting a known technique transplanted to point sets.
- A source-only Adam-STE search with 16 decoder evaluations beats the learned refiner by 6.3% (val) / 9.7% (OOD) using exactly the same information. This undermines the 'semi-amortized learned inference' contribution: why train a refiner at all? It is currently a 16-cloud diagnostic, not a baseline; run at full scale it is the paper's strongest competitor and must appear in every table.
- Baseline set is inadequate for a 'beats point selection' claim: only deterministic FPS (extremal-start, known-weak at K=8) and an internal feature codec at the headline point. Missing: k-means/Lloyd centroids (the obvious non-learned free-coordinate encoder at identical rate), random/best-of-N subsets (which beat FPS by 7.9% in Exp 004), Poisson-disk, SampleNet/APES/LighTN-style learned samplers through the same frozen decoder, and G-PCC-low-rate output + a PU-Net/Grad-PU upsampler to separate decoder from refiner contribution.
- The representation claim (coordinates > feature latent, +29.9%) is confounded by unequal training protocol: feature codec trained 2 epochs, no EMA, no calibration selection, 256 updates (Exp 018) vs constellation 4 epochs + EMA + calibration (Exp 019); the like-for-like comparison in Exp 018 failed (-6.97%, CI -29.23..10.78). The comparator is also home-grown and fixed-width; no published feature-latent method (e.g., an entropy-coded PointNet/FoldingNet AE, D-PCC-style) is included.
- Evaluation set and protocol: 672-mesh ModelNet40 subset (512 train / 128 val / 32 OOD), not full ModelNet40 test (2,468) or ShapeNetCore (blocked); the 'validation' split is the official ModelNet40 test split and the same 160 clouds drove go/no-go and protocol redesigns across Exp 016-020 (019's protocol was designed after 018's failure on it). There is no untouched final test slice. The OOD split is 32 clouds, giving CIs that cross zero in several cells.
- Only one published learned codec was attempted (pcc_geo_cnn_v2, 2020, TF1) and only via a self-adapted q6/global low-rate protocol with two compatibility patches; the 5k retrain collapsed (constant 803 B, empty decodes) and the single valid 20k-step overlapping point (46 B, val D1 645.97 vs constellation 264.41) is cluster-only, unrecorded in docs or issue #15, and single-lambda. Gate B (>=3 overlapping points) is open. No modern codec (PCGCv2, SparsePCGC, AnyPcc, UniPCGC) at any rate, and a leaked-checkpoint q6 probe already shows pcc_geo_cnn_v2 at 111 B / 36 dB D1 vs the constellation's ~28.6 dB at 50 B.
- Prior-art coverage is admitted to be incomplete (landscape doc: formal review required). Expected but uncited: point-based learned PCC (Yan 2019, Huang & Liu 2019, Wiesmann RA-L 2021, IPDAE, Pointsoup), learned samplers (APES, LighTN, DA-Net), upsamplers (PU-Net/PU-GAN/PU-GCN/Grad-PU) which constitute the decoder half, skeleton/keypoint latents (Point2Skeleton, SkeletonNet, USIP), implicit codecs (COIN/COIN++, NPCF), Set Transformer/TSPN/Perceiver, and the encoder-side RD-optimization line above.
- Gate B (geometry vs private code) was never executed on procedural data: all 001-013 Chamfer numbers are against the same 256-point sample fed to the encoder; free coordinates sit ~0.08 RMSE off the sample with no analytic surface-distance check, despite analytic normals being available. The anti-steganography argument rests on ModelNet40 fresh-resampling only.

**Minor**
- serialized_round_trip_exact / coordinates_on_exact_lattice contract checks are computed on the decoded packet and cannot fail; do not cite them as evidence.
- Per-mesh normalization (center + unit-radius scale, 4 floats) is not in any stream; state this and the encoder-side decoder access / 40 ms vs 2.35 ms encode cost explicitly in the rate/complexity section.

## Claim and novelty characterization — Point Constellation

### (1) Central hypothesis as currently phrased
Two layers coexist in the docs:

- **Original / representation-level** (`README.md`, `docs/experiment-plan.md` "Research question"): *At equal actual coded rate, can an unordered, quantized `K x 3` constellation decoded by a shared model reconstruct point-cloud geometry as well as or better than established codecs and simpler sampling baselines?* Strictness is defined by `docs/representation-contract.md`: no per-point features, normals, order, topology, per-cloud weights, or sub-quantization perturbations; permutation invariance mandatory; quantization + ~half-bin jitter + surface-proximity loss as anti-steganography controls.
- **Current / mechanism-level** (`docs/month-1-paper-viability-sprint.md` L9-14): *"an unordered, quantized `K x 3` same-space latent can outperform ordinary point selection because semi-amortized recurrent inference finds decoder-useful geometric constellations."* The unit of comparison is now **FPS through the same frozen decoder**, not a codec.

### (2) How the claim evolved

`docs/research-landscape.md` (six families): G-PCC, Draco, V-PCC; Learned-PCGC, VoxelContext-Net, Density-Preserving PCC, JPEG Pleno Part 6; Implicit Autoencoder, LINR-PCGC; SampleNet, Feature-Preserving Simplification, PCS-Net; plane/quadric/polygon-cloud primitive codecs; Irregular Convolutional AE, Sparse Latent Point Diffusion; Superpoint Network/Graphs (disambiguation). `docs/co-adaptation-hypotheses.md` adds inference-side art: Iterative Amortized Inference (Marino), Semi-Amortized VAE (Kim), Improving Inference for Neural Image Compression (Yang 2020), DeepSDF, DSPN, Slot Attention, DN-DETR, S-NET, SQ-VAE, Lewis-game emergent communication, autoencoder-grounded MARL, TTUR. Exp 019/020 name AnyPcc, UniPCGC, SparsePCGC, PCGCv2/`pcc_geo_cnn_v2` as comparators.

Novelty boundary drawn (landscape table): the *strict combination* — entire latent is an unordered quantized 3D coordinate set with no feature channels, decoded by a shared generative decoder, with counted bitstream — is "a novelty hypothesis, not a novelty claim." Simplification methods are called "the strongest coordinate-only ablations" but lack code semantics/bitstream; latent-point models keep feature channels; primitive codecs need typed metadata. Four-corner plane is explicitly disclaimed as not novel.

### (5) What kind of claim, and what is best supported
- **Compression claim**: *not* supported. Docs are explicit (README "Claims about competitive general-purpose compression performance have not been made"); G-PCC dominates above ~0.24 bpp; no overlapping rate with a published learned codec; only one 50-byte operating point is stabilized; entropy coding and variable-K are deferred.
- **Representation claim** (coordinate-only ≥ feature latent at matched bytes): moderately supported — Exp 019 29.90% (CI 23.26-35.77) vs an *internal*, fixed-width, self-built feature codec; official D2 favors coordinates. Weak because the comparator is home-grown, single rate point, ModelNet40 subset (512 train meshes).
- **Inference claim** (semi-amortized competitive refinement with frozen-decoder gradients finds better constellations than FPS through the same decoder): **best supported** — replicated across procedural (3 seeds, Exp 013), ModelNet40 (6 decoders x 3 refiners, Exp 019), official D1/D2 (Exp 020), fresh resampling, category OOD, all decoder marginals positive. Caveats: the mechanism is dominated by decoder-gradient feedback (5% vs 23%), it is a *relative* gain over FPS with a decoder trained on FPS-style messages, and the recurrent refiner sits only 6.3-9.7% above a per-cloud Adam bound, so the "learned amortization" novelty is thin relative to "encoder-side optimization through a frozen decoder," which is well-trodden in neural image compression.

**Key facts**
- Sprint doc (docs/month-1-paper-viability-sprint.md L9-14) phrases the central claim as: a quantized unordered K x 3 same-space latent outperforms ordinary point selection because semi-amortized recurrent inference finds decoder-useful constellations.
- Original research question (docs/experiment-plan.md L5-8) is a codec claim at equal actual coded rate vs established codecs and sampling baselines.
- Representation contract (docs/representation-contract.md) forbids features/normals/order/topology/per-cloud weights/sub-bin perturbations; requires quantization-in-loop, half-bin jitter, surface-proximity loss.
- Exp 005: refiner with input-only decoder gradient 0.18261 -> 0.14073 (22.94%); no-gradient arm only 0.17334 (5.07%) — decoder-gradient feedback dominates the mechanism.
- Exp 013: 3 seeds, N=256 K=16 q=12, 23.33% over FPS (CI 20.44-26.62) validation, 25.13% OOD.
- Exp 017-018 ModelNet40 K=8 0.1953 bpp: +16.13% over FPS (CI 13.49-19.85); G-PCC overtakes by ~0.24-0.27 bpp; internal feature codec not reliably beaten (-6.97%, CI -29.23..10.78).
- Exp 019: decoder seed explains 99.998% of variance; stabilized 6x3 factorial beats byte-matched feature codec 29.90% (CI 23.26-35.77) at exactly 50 bytes; refiner within 6.3-9.7% of per-cloud Adam bound.
- Exp 020: official D1 +16.52% / D2 +34.65% over FPS; pcc_geo_cnn_v2 released checkpoints 35-176x above 50-byte rate; retrained low-rate pilots produced empty decodes — no overlapping rate point exists.
- docs/research-landscape.md self-describes novelty as 'a novelty hypothesis, not a novelty claim' and says a formal literature review is required before publication.
- Cited inference-side art: Marino iterative amortized inference, Kim semi-amortized VAE, Yang 2020 improving inference for neural image compression, DeepSDF, DSPN, Slot Attention, DN-DETR, S-NET, SampleNet, SQ-VAE, Lewis-game emergent communication, TTUR.

**Weaknesses**
- Compression framing is not supported: single stabilized operating point (50 bytes), G-PCC dominates above ~0.24 bpp, no overlapping-rate learned-codec comparison, entropy coding and adaptive K deferred.
- Feature-codec comparator in Exp 018/019 is an internal, fixed-width, self-built baseline; a reviewer will not accept it as evidence that coordinates beat feature latents in general.
- The mechanism's gain is mostly frozen-decoder gradient feedback (encoder-side optimization), which is established practice in neural image compression (Yang 2020, Campos, van Rozendaal); the learned recurrent/competitive component adds only ~5% and sits 6-10% from a per-cloud Adam bound, so the 'semi-amortized' novelty is thin.
- All comparisons are relative to FPS through a decoder trained on FPS-style messages; absolute quality vs modern codecs (AnyPcc, SparsePCGC, UniPCGC) is untested.
- Data scale is small (512 ModelNet40 training meshes, 2,048 points, 128 validation, 32 category-OOD); ShapeNetCore pending.
- The prior-art map omits several point-based learned codecs, learned samplers (APES, LighTN), upsamplers (PU-Net family), skeleton/keypoint latents, and encoder-side RD optimization literature; the docs themselves concede a formal review is outstanding.
- The uncited-prior-work list in this summary is from memory and unverified.

## Code-level methodology audit (branch agent/experiment-020-external-codec)

### (a) Target visibility — FINE, with one framing concern
- The refiner's encoder-side decoder-gradient probe (`src/pointconstellation/models/refiner.py:157-183`) computes Chamfer of `decoder(quantized)` against a `target` argument. In every evaluation/training call the target is the encoder input itself: `stability_experiment.py:706` (`target=source`), `:822-827` (`target=source`), `official_stability.py:391-396` (`target=source`). The held-out resample (`fresh_points`, `data/mesh.py:323`, drawn with a distinct role seed at `:290-300`) is only used for the `fresh_chamfer_mse` column (`stability_experiment.py:820,873`). The Adam probe scores on `probe_source` only (`:843-851`). No path lets the encoder see the fresh target. `run_pc_error` also uses `source` as reference (`official_stability.py:415-421`), which is the normal compression convention.
- Concern: the encoder is decoder-aware (needs the frozen decoder at encode time, `refiner.py:168-180`). This is legitimate but must be stated as an encoder-complexity asymmetry vs. FPS.

### (b) Exact quantization / STE — FINE, but the "exactness" flags are vacuous
- Training: `quantize_ste` with half-bin jitter + straight-through (`quantization.py:31-47`); refiner uses `training=self.training, jitter=False` (`refiner.py:217-219, 254-256`), Adam probe uses STE with `training=True, jitter=False` (`gradient_free.py:279-281`). Eval: modules are `.eval()`-ed (`stability_experiment.py:674, 732`), so `quantize_ste(..., training=False)` is a hard round, then every message is serialized/deserialized through `encode_constellation`/`decode_constellation` before decoding (`stability_experiment.py:760-800`, `official_stability.py:398-402, 411`). Reconstruction is always produced from the decoded stream. Good.
- Concern: `serialized_round_trip_exact` and `coordinates_on_exact_lattice` (`stability_experiment.py:775-793`) are computed on the *decoded* packet, which is on-lattice by construction (`bitstream.py:153-154`), and re-encoding decoded values trivially reproduces the stream. These checks cannot fail; the contract report (`official_metrics.json: contract_checks`) advertises them as guarantees. Not a correctness bug, but the paper should not cite them as evidence.

### (c) Bitstream accounting — FINE with a normalization caveat
- `expected_stream_bytes = HEADER.size + ceil(3*K*bits/8)` with 14-byte header `>4sBBBBHI` (magic, version, mode, bits, domain, K, output N) (`bitstream.py:21,38-45`). K=8, q=12 → 36 + 14 = 50. So K, precision, N and mode are all counted; no entropy coding, so bytes are real. Feature-codec baseline header is 12 bytes (`feature_bitstream.py:14,28-33`); 38 dims × 8 bits = 38 B → 50 B, byte-matched (`stability_experiment.py:1485-1487, 1543-1544` enforce equality).
- Concern: per-mesh normalization (center to bbox midpoint, scale to unit radius, `data/mesh.py:152-161`) is applied before everything and its 4 floats are not in any stream. All arms (feature codec, pcc_geo_cnn_v2, G-PCC) receive the same normalized source, so comparisons are consistent, but the absolute "50 bytes" claim is in the normalized frame.

### (d) FPS baseline fairness — FINE on mechanics; weak on baseline choice
- Same frozen decoder (hash-checked before/after, `stability_experiment.py:674-675, 729-731`; `official_stability.py:328-335`), same 12-bit quantization (`refiner_experiment.py:176-195` ends with `quantize_ste(..., training=False)`), same 50-byte stream (size-consistency guard `official_stability.py:553-556`). The decoder is trained *only* on FPS constellations (`stability_experiment.py:518-524`), which if anything favors FPS.
- FPS is deterministic (start = farthest-from-centroid, `refiner_experiment.py:179`); deterministic FPS at K=8 on 2048 points picks extremal points and is a known-weak reconstruction anchor.
- Concern (reviewer-facing): at the headline point the only baselines are FPS and an internal feature codec. Random subsets exist only in old experiments (`bottleneck_audit.py:26-27`, docs/experiment-004 shows "best of FPS + 8 random" beat FPS by 7.9%). There is no k-means/Lloyd centroid (the natural non-learned *free-coordinate* baseline at identical rate), no Poisson-disk, no SampleNet-style learned sampler. More damaging: the source-only Adam search (`gradient_free.py:256-302`, 16 decoder evals) is *better* than the learned refiner (docs/experiment-019: refiner is 6.3%/9.7% above the Adam bound). Since Adam-STE search uses exactly the same information as the refiner, a reviewer will ask why a learned refiner is needed at all; the paper must present Adam search as a full-scale baseline, not a 16-cloud diagnostic.

### (e) Calibration / sealed selection — FINE within 019/020; meta-level concern
- Calibration split is 128 official-train meshes disjoint from train/validation/OOD (`mesh_manifest.py:206-233`; membership overlap check `stability_experiment.py:1659-1661`; category checks `:339-370`). Selection uses only FPS Chamfer on calibration (`:455-483`, `:554-585`). Official run verifies the selection record contains no test-named keys (`official_stability.py:89-99, 316-319`) — that is a key-name check only, cosmetic.
- All six decoders picked the same epoch-4 EMA candidate, so "selection" did nothing; the stabilization effect is longer training + EMA.
- Concern: the "validation" split is the official ModelNet40 *test* split (`mesh_manifest.py:213-233`), and the same 128+32 clouds have driven go/no-go and protocol decisions across experiments 016→020 (e.g. 019's protocol was designed after 018's validation failure). No single run peeks, but the experiment sequence is iterative tuning on the reported set. Needs either a truly untouched final test slice or explicit disclosure.
- Concern: feature-codec baseline (experiment 018: 2 epochs, no EMA, no calibration selection, `configs/experiment_018_feature_codec_multiseed.json`) is compared against the stabilized constellation (4 epochs + EMA). The 29.9% "learned-codec gate" is confounded by unequal training protocol.

### (f) Statistics — implementation sane; sample sizes fragile
- `official_stability._bootstrap_comparison` (`:141-215`): two-stage hierarchical draw (categories with replacement, then clouds within category, `:129-139`), decoder indices shared between FPS and refiner arrays (paired), refiner indices independent, ratio computed on sqrt of pooled MSE. Correct pairing; percentile CI. Test at `tests/test_official_stability.py:30-48` only checks a synthetic positive case.

### (1) Where the constellation wins/loses vs G-PCC
- **Win corridor ≈ 0.19–0.25 bpp (46–62 bytes).** Stabilized K=8 at 0.195 bpp beats G-PCC at 0.2394 bpp on Chamfer (0.109 vs 0.134), D1 (28.6 vs 27.0 dB) and marginally D2 (32.4 vs 32.0). Interpolating, matched-distortion rate saving is ~20–28%, and it is *entirely* inside one octree depth step of G-PCC.
- **Loss from ≈0.27 bpp upward.** G-PCC at 0.2729 bpp (70 bytes) already dominates every stabilized cell on all three metrics (0.106 vs 0.109 Chamfer; 29.1 vs 28.6 dB D1; 34.6 vs 32.4 D2). Exp 017 K=16 (0.336 bpp, 0.140) and K=32 (0.617 bpp, 0.128) lose to G-PCC at lower rate by 1.3× and 2.3× in RMSE; `gpcc_measured_point_dominates=True` for all K≥16 rows.
- **Below 0.18 bpp (K=4, 32 bytes) there is no G-PCC comparator at all**; the "win" there is by default.
- **Header confound:** the constellation's 14-byte header is 28% of its stream; G-PCC's ~40-byte parameter sets are ~65% of its 62-byte stream. Payload-only, G-PCC spends ~21 bytes for 53 points (0.134) versus 36 bytes for 8 constellation points (0.109). A header-normalised or sequence-amortised comparison would shrink or erase the corridor. No experiment has done this.

### (2) Is ~0.2 bpp / 50 bytes a recognised regime?
No. MPEG G-PCC/V-PCC CTCs (8iVFB, MVUB, Owlii, Ford/Qnxadas) evaluate 10^5–10^6-point frames at 0.05–10 bpp with D1 PSNR 55–75 dB; the bpp overlaps numerically but the absolute streams are tens of KB and the distortion regime (28 dB on a 12-bit grid) is two orders of magnitude worse. pcc_geo_cnn/v2 evaluate ModelNet40 at ~1–10+ bpp; PCGCv2/SparsePCGC/OctAttention/AnyPcc use the MPEG/LiDAR sets. Fifty bytes per object is descriptor-scale (a 1024-d fp32 shape code is 4 KB), so this is an ultra-low-rate "shape abstraction" niche the codec literature does not evaluate. The docs already concede `mpeg_common_test_conditions=false` and "not the full official ModelNet40 test set".

### (3) Codecs that could plausibly be pushed to overlapping rates
- **G-PCC itself with stripped/amortised parameter sets, or a learned octree entropy model (OctAttention, VoxelContext-Net, EHEM) at depth 4–6:** almost certainly lands in 0.15–0.25 bpp and would attack the corridor directly. This is the most dangerous missing baseline.
- **pcc_geo_cnn_v2 q6-global with large λ:** already at 111 bytes/36 dB on one cloud; a converged retrain (≥40k steps, upstream ≈39k) at λ≥1e-5 with a 32³ grid could reach 60–90 bytes. Retraining at 5k steps collapsed; this is a training-budget failure, not a codec verdict.
- **PCGCv2/SparsePCGC:** base layer is G-PCC-lossless-coded coarse voxels + entropy-coded features; at 2048 points voxelised to 6–7 bits, plausible 0.3–1 bpp; below that the base-layer bytes floor them. Feasible with CUDA/MinkowskiEngine on EmpireAI.
- **Draco:** kd-tree coder at qb=3–5 bits still costs ≥1–2 bits/point on 2048 points; only reaches 50 bytes if points are pre-decimated to K — i.e. the FPS/SampleNet-to-K + Draco baseline, which is precisely the comparison the paper should run (learned simplification → standard coder → learned upsampler).
- **Decoder-side upsampling of G-PCC's 50-point output (0.24 bpp)** with a PU-Net-style model isolates the shared decoder's contribution from the refiner's.

### (4) What is missing for a defensible RD figure
1. Only **one stabilized rate point (K=8)** exists; Exp 017's 4-point curve is unstable across seeds and only K=4,8 lie below the crossover. Need stabilized K∈{4,6,8,10,12,16}×q∈{8,10,12} with official D1/D2 on all cells.
2. **No entropy coding**: 12-bit raw coordinates; sorting + delta or coding the K points with G-PCC/octree could cut the 36-byte payload 20–40%; q=8 alone gives 38 bytes.
3. **No variable K / rate control**; encoder is fixed-K.
4. **Header-normalised accounting** (payload-only and full-stream) for both sides.
5. ≥5 overlapping points per method and Bjøntegaard BD-rate/BD-PSNR with seed CIs; the docs correctly refuse BD-rate now.
6. Fair converged external learned codec (any of the above), full ModelNet40 test (2,468 meshes) and ShapeNet; encode cost (40 ms vs 2 ms for the feature codec) reported alongside.

### Verdict
Not a codec paper. The RD evidence is one rate point, in a ≈0.19–0.25 bpp window that G-PCC's fixed syntax partly manufactures, with G-PCC winning by >2× RMSE at 0.45 bpp and a 111-byte pcc_geo_cnn_v2 diagnostic already at 36 dB D1. The robust, CI-backed findings are representational: a K×3 coordinate-only latent chosen by semi-amortised recurrent refinement beats FPS through the same frozen decoder (+16.5% D1, +34.7% D2, 6/6 decoders) and beats a byte-matched learned feature latent (+29.9%, CI 23–36). That is a **representation / learned-simplification paper** ("coordinate-only bottleneck with a shared geometric decoder"), with an amortised-inference sub-result (refiner within 6–10% of per-cloud Adam). Rate-distortion vs G-PCC belongs as a positioning/sanity figure with header-normalised bars, not as the headline claim.

**Weaknesses**
- The constellation-vs-G-PCC corridor is confounded by fixed syntax overhead: G-PCC's ~40-byte parameter sets dominate its 46-62-byte streams while the constellation header is 14 bytes; no header-normalised or sequence-amortised comparison exists.
- Only one stabilized rate point (K=8); the multi-K curve comes from the unstable Exp 017 model, so no RD curve, no BD-rate, and only 2 points below the crossover.
- No entropy coding or variable-K rate control on the constellation side, so its own rate is not optimised either.
- No published learned codec overlaps the rate; the only external quality number is a single leaked-checkpoint cloud at 111 bytes that beats the constellation by ~8 dB D1 at 2.2x rate.
- The most dangerous baselines (learned octree entropy models at low depth, FPS/SampleNet + Draco/G-PCC, G-PCC low-rate output + learned upsampler) have not been run.
- Above ~0.27 bpp the method loses decisively (2.3x RMSE at 0.45-0.62 bpp); the operating regime (28 dB D1 on a 12-bit grid) is far below any standard lossy test condition.
- D1/D2 PSNR values for the stabilized model were derived here from grid-unit RMSE using the peak implied by G-PCC rows (~7.1e3), not read directly from a PSNR field.
- Evaluation set is a 672-mesh ModelNet40 subset; ShapeNet and full ModelNet40 test are untested.
