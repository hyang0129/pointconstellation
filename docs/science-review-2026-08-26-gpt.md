# Decision memo: Point Constellation paper strategy

**Decision:** Target **NeurIPS 2027** with an **amortized-inference** paper. Do not submit the current work to ICLR 2027 main track: the actual paper deadline is September 25, 2026, not approximately October 1, leaving insufficient time to obtain an untouched test, decisive optimization controls, and a stabilized rate curve.

## 1. State of the science

Point Constellation currently supports a credible but narrow inference result. Given a frozen shared completion decoder, recurrent source-conditioned refinement finds an unordered, quantized coordinate message that is substantially better than byte-matched FPS through that same decoder. The strongest operating point is ModelNet40 at \(N=2048,K=8,q=12\): an independently decodable 50-byte stream, or 0.1953125 bpp, with positive official D1/D2 effects for all six decoder seeds. This is not yet a codec result because only one rate is stabilized, the evaluation records influenced method development, normalization metadata is omitted, the strongest comparator is FPS, and no published learned codec has a valid overlapping curve. Gate A passes at the procedural 12-bit point, Gate C passes only for the ModelNet40 substitute, while geometry Gate B, codec Gate D, and inference-headroom Gate E remain open.

### Reconciliation of disputed reports

- Experiment 017’s category-OOD gain is 18.1846%; **18.18% and 18.19% are rounding variants**.
- The 42.73% Q90 stabilization number exists in the artifact, but a 90th percentile and narrow bootstrap interval from six decoder marginals is not defensible tail inference. Report the six marginals, median, and maximum descriptively instead.
- Experiment 020’s **275.22 s** is the initial official evaluation; the artifact’s **25.65 s** describes a resumed/aggregation run. Both need explicit labels.
- The untracked benchmark plan claims a valid 46-byte, 20k-step `pcc_geo_cnn_v2` result. Tracked documentation, the checked configuration, and local artifacts contain only the failed 5k result and the 20k plan. The 46-byte result is therefore **unverified cluster-only state** and must not enter the paper until its checkpoint, streams, per-cloud metrics, and hashes are preserved and regenerated.

## 4. Recommended framing

**Choose: amortized inference.**

“Codec” is not supported by the current comparison set. “Representation plus learned simplification” is inaccurate because the headline message is free-coordinate and the subset result is post-hoc. The distinctive supported question is how to infer a tiny, discrete, exchangeable coordinate message for a fixed decoder.

### Draft title

**Finding the Message: Competitive Recurrent Inference for Quantized Coordinate-Set Point-Cloud Bottlenecks**

### Three-sentence abstract

We study inference for a frozen point-cloud decoder when the complete per-object message is an unordered, quantized set of coordinates in the source frame. On a pre-normalized ModelNet40 protocol, a competitive recurrent refiner initialized from FPS produces an exact 50-byte \(K=8\) stream and reduces official D1/D2 RMSE by 16.5%/34.7% on the selected validation slice and 18.8%/33.8% on held-out categories relative to FPS through the same decoder. These results establish an inference improvement under a strict coordinate bottleneck, while motivating compute-matched, multi-rate, and geometric-semantics tests needed to distinguish useful spatial inference from decoder-private coding.

## 5. Paper outline with figures and tables

1. **Introduction and claim boundary.** Present the amortization gap under a coordinate-only set bottleneck; compression is motivation and evaluation context, not the central claim.

2. **Problem formulation and theory.** Define unordered set/multiset messages, actual serialized rate, permutation invariance, equivariance, and private-code ambiguity. Formal targets should include the capacity of a \(K\)-multiset over a finite 3D lattice and the difference between \(3Kq\) fixed-width cost and the information capacity of an unordered set.

3. **Competitive recurrent inference.** Frozen decoder, FPS initialization, source-only decoder gradients, competitive responsibility allocation, recurrent quantized updates, and canonical serialization.

4. **Mechanism and amortization gap.** Compare learned recurrence with no-feedback recurrence and direct optimization under matched decoder evaluations and wall clock.

5. **Rate-distortion and baseline evaluation.** Stable multi-rate coordinate curve, feature latent, selection/simplification controls, G-PCC, and published learned codecs.

6. **Geometry or private protocol?** Analytic procedural tests, fresh resampling, perturbations, cross-decoder transfer, rigid transforms, boundary/thin structures, and anchor visualizations.

7. **Generalization, complexity, and limitations.** Untouched ModelNet, external data, model amortization, latency, memory, normalization cost, failure cases, and crossover to raw/G-PCC coding.

## 8. Risks and kill criteria

| Risk | Kill or reframe criterion |
|---|---|
| The refiner is only expensive truncated optimization. | If it is not on the time–quality Pareto frontier against Adam/STE or strong search at any budget, drop “learned recurrent inference” as the headline; present it only as an amortized approximation if it offers a measured speed advantage. |
| FPS created an artificially easy baseline. | If k-means, Poisson, curvature-aware selection, or random best-of-\(B\) matches the refiner within the primary CI, stop claiming a distinctive inference gain. |
| Coordinates are a decoder-private protocol. | If Gate B gains vanish on \(X_c\)/analytic metrics, anchors are far off-surface, one-bin perturbations are catastrophic, or cross-decoder transfer fails, drop “spatial representation” and call the message a constrained coordinate code. |

| The low-rate G-PCC corridor is an artifact. | If stabilized coordinate points are dominated after identical-source, header-aware G-PCC reruns, remove codec competitiveness from the title, abstract, and main claims. |
| No published learned codec reaches overlap. | Follow Issue #15’s stop rule, attempt one modern compatible method, then stop retraining if neither yields three valid overlapping points. Report the nearest-point gap without extrapolation. |
| Untouched or external performance collapses. | If the primary effect is nonpositive on the untouched ModelNet set or absent on all external sets, restrict the work to a ModelNet/CAD-prior observation; do not submit as a general geometry result. |
| Multi-rate training remains unstable. | If fewer than five stable points are obtained or seed variance overwhelms the primary effect, make the paper fixed-rate inference only; without decisive mechanism evidence, stop the main-track submission. |
| Rate accounting removes the 50-byte story. | If required normalization/state metadata materially shifts the crossover, use full end-to-end bytes as primary and describe 50 bytes only as a normalized-domain diagnostic. |
| Novelty collapses under close prior art. | If KeyPointDiffuser, Key-Grid, or prior compression-inference work already satisfies the same strict payload and inference contract, require a new formal or empirical principle before submission. |
| Statistical replication remains too shallow. | Do not use Q90 or population-level bad-seed claims from six decoders; if three refiner seeds drive unstable conclusions, add seeds or narrow the estimand. |

The paper survives as a NeurIPS-quality inference contribution only if three things hold simultaneously: the refiner remains useful against compute-matched optimization and strong coordinate baselines, the effect confirms on untouched/external data, and the stabilized multi-rate evaluation establishes that the phenomenon is not a single-point artifact.

# Appendix A: adversarial reviews


## Reviewer: novelty

## Score

**4/10 — Weak reject**

The work contains a real, unusually well-audited result, but the evidence does not yet support a NeurIPS-level compression claim, and the inference contribution is insufficiently isolated from known semi-amortized optimization ideas.

## Verdict

Reject in the current form. I would encourage resubmission as an **amortized-inference paper under a coordinate-only set bottleneck**, after stronger controls and an untouched evaluation. The 50-byte result is interesting: across six decoders and three refiners, it improves official D1/D2 over matched FPS, with exact serialization and no demonstrated target leakage. But one stabilized operating point against FPS is not a codec paper, and “reconstructing shapes from sparse learned keypoints” is already occupied territory. The submission currently sits awkwardly between representation, inference, and compression papers without fully closing any one of them.

## Best honest framing

This paper studies **competitive semi-amortized inference of an unordered, quantized, same-space coordinate message for a frozen shared point-cloud decoder**. At one pre-normalized ModelNet40 operating point—\(N=2048,K=8,q=12\), exactly 50 bytes—the learned recurrent inference procedure finds free-coordinate constellations that reconstruct significantly better than FPS through the same decoder, including under official MPEG D1/D2 and fresh surface resampling. The contribution is the constrained inference problem and its empirical solution, not a generally competitive codec, learned sampling method, proof that coordinates remain semantically geometric, or discovery that sparse keypoints can condition shape generation.

## Strengths

- The representation contract is unusually rigorous: coordinate-only payload, canonical unordered serialization, exact quantization, frozen-decoder checks, and explicit separation of free coordinates, strict subsets, and post-hoc projections.

- The primary result is credible within its narrow scope. The six-decoder-by-three-refiner evaluation reports validation gains of 16.52% D1 and 34.65% D2 over matched FPS, with all decoder marginals positive and exact 50-byte round trips ([Experiment 020](/Users/hong/code/pointconstellation/docs/experiment-020-official-and-published-codec.md:10)).

- FPS is evaluated favorably: it uses the same decoder, rate, quantizer, and serialization, and the decoder was trained on FPS inputs.

- The authors preserve negative results, invalid target-assisted runs, collapsed external-codec training, and distinctions between smoke tests and replicated evidence. That scientific honesty materially raises confidence.

- Fresh resampling and category OOD reduce the chance that the result is merely memorizing the encoder-visible point sample.

- The narrow low-rate comparison with G-PCC is legitimately intriguing. The stabilized 0.1953-bpp point appears to dominate several measured G-PCC points before G-PCC crosses over around 0.24–0.27 bpp. This deserves investigation, although not yet a curve-level claim.

## Fatal or major issues (most severe first)

1. **The submission is not currently a competitive compression paper.** Only \(K=8\) has the stabilized protocol. The official headline compares only the refiner and FPS at one rate. There is no valid five-point D1/D2 curve, no BD-rate result, no common test condition, and no overlapping published learned-codec curve. G-PCC wins beyond the narrow low-rate corridor. Modern codecs such as UniPCGC explicitly support entropy coding, variable rate, and full RD evaluation; that is the evidentiary standard this paper must meet ([UniPCGC](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.18541)).

2. **The novelty is a combination of known ingredients, not yet a demonstrated new principle.** Sparse learned keypoints conditioning shape reconstruction already appear in Key-Grid and KeyPointDiffuser. Key-Grid additionally passes encoder-layer information to its decoder, while KeyPointDiffuser concatenates auxiliary latent features, so neither satisfies this paper’s strict contract—but they remove any novelty from the broad “coordinates/keypoints reconstruct a shape” story ([Key-Grid](https://papers.nips.cc/paper_files/paper/2024/hash/582e9771ac8527cb6390e5e9444a0fee-Abstract-Conference.html), [KeyPointDiffuser](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.03450)). Likewise, hybrid amortized/iterative inference is established in semi-amortized VAEs and neural compression ([SAVI](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v80/kim18e.html), [Improving Inference for Neural Image Compression](https://arxiv.org/abs/2006.04240)). The novelty therefore rests entirely on applying competitive recurrent inference to a strict coordinate-only codec. That may be publishable, but only with decisive mechanism evidence.

3. **There is no untouched confirmatory test.** ModelNet official-test records labeled validation and category OOD influenced the stability gates and advancement of the stabilized protocol. Experiment 020 changes the metric, but evaluates the already-selected method on the same records. Calibration-only checkpoint selection is clean; experiment-level method selection is not. The reported intervals are valid descriptive intervals for these clouds, not evidence from an unopened test set.

4. **FPS is too weak to establish the value of the learned refiner.** The refiner receives decoder gradients, recurrence, an FPS initialization, and roughly 17× the encoding time. It must be compared against identical-byte k-means/Lloyd, Poisson-disk or blue-noise sampling, voxel/grid representatives, curvature-aware simplification, random best-of-\(B\), learned sampling such as SampleNet, and direct per-cloud latent optimization. SampleNet already established task-aware learned point selection for reconstruction ([SampleNet](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content_CVPR_2020/html/Lang_SampleNet_Differentiable_Point_Cloud_Sampling_CVPR_2020_paper.html)). The existing Adam probe being 6.3%/9.7% better than the refiner suggests the primary phenomenon may simply be approximate encoder-side optimization.

5. **The spatial-semantics claim is unresolved.** The winning messages are free coordinates. Fresh-target reconstruction shows that the decoder learned a shape prior; it does not show that the anchors correspond to the underlying surface rather than a decoder-private control protocol. The strict result is only post-hoc projection, not a trained strict-subset encoder. Gate B—analytic surface distance, perturbation robustness, boundary/thin-structure behavior, and independent \(X_a/X_b/X_c\) sampling—remains open.

6. **The 50-byte rate is not yet an end-to-end original-coordinate codec rate.** Mesh center and scale are derived from the complete mesh but not serialized ([mesh.py](/Users/hong/code/pointconstellation/src/pointconstellation/data/mesh.py:152)). The claim is valid only under a shared pre-normalized benchmark domain. The 405,394-byte shared model, amortization assumptions, and substantially slower encoder also matter to practical positioning.

7. **The mechanism remains entangled.** Recurrence, competitive responsibility assignment, decoder-gradient feedback, FPS initialization, quantization-aware updates, decoder stabilization, and additional compute have not been cleanly factorially ablated in the stabilized real-data regime. The older no-gradient result is suggestive but does not establish which parts are necessary now.

8. **Generalization evidence is too narrow for a strong learned-geometry claim.** The evidence covers 128 ModelNet validation objects and 32 held-out-category objects, not full ModelNet40, ShapeNetCore, or MPEG/JPEG content. A powerful shared decoder may exploit a small normalized-object prior. DiffCom, for example, evaluates sparse-prior compression on ShapeNet and MPEG datasets, illustrating the expected breadth ([DiffCom](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.13860)).

## Reader: claims

## Summary

**Current central hypothesis.** The repository’s paper-level hypothesis is phrased most directly as: at equal **actual coded rate**, can an unordered, quantized \(K\times3\) constellation, decoded by a shared model, reconstruct geometry as well as or better than established codecs and simple sampling baselines? ([experiment-plan.md:3–7](/Users/hong/code/pointconstellation/docs/experiment-plan.md:3)). This is stricter than “a sparse point set can summarize a dense one.” The complete learned per-cloud payload must be quantized coordinates in the source coordinate system—no features, topology, meaningful order, primitive IDs, implicit fields, or per-instance weights—and every varying decoding parameter must be charged to the rate ([representation-contract.md:8–27](/Users/hong/code/pointconstellation/docs/representation-contract.md:8)). The intended semantics are also stronger than mere coordinate-shaped bits: anchors should remain spatially meaningful under quantization, jitter, visualization, and surface-proximity tests; otherwise the result is classified as a private coordinate codebook ([representation-contract.md:42–56](/Users/hong/code/pointconstellation/docs/representation-contract.md:42)).

**Evolution.** The project began with an analytic four-corner plane example, explicitly only an API/representation sanity check, not the learned contribution ([README.md:42–51](/Users/hong/code/pointconstellation/README.md:42)). The first learned story was conventional end-to-end amortization: a permutation-invariant encoder proposed \(K\) coordinates and a PointNet/FoldingNet-style shared decoder expanded them ([ml-architecture.md:17–39](/Users/hong/code/pointconstellation/docs/ml-architecture.md:17)). That story weakened empirically. The initial model beat matched FPS by 4.29% at one 576-bit point, but its curve was essentially flat from 96 to 1,152 payload bits ([README.md:123–151](/Users/hong/code/pointconstellation/README.md:123)); relation-aware decoding restored rate utilization while learned anchors lost to FPS, and the hard learned subset was still 28.12% worse ([README.md:153–180](/Users/hong/code/pointconstellation/README.md:153)). Experiment 004 then froze a multi-rate decoder: the learned subset was 41.65% worse than FPS, yet separately optimized free coordinates beat the best sampled subset by 15.60%, albeit with an unpaired initialization and only finite-sample surface distance ([README.md:182–197](/Users/hong/code/pointconstellation/README.md:182)).

That failure shifted the leading hypothesis from **representation discovery by one-shot autoencoding** to **constellation inference**. The working synthesis is that useful messages require conditional search, broad/searchable decoder basins, and competitive allocation of distinct anchor responsibilities ([co-adaptation-hypotheses.md:283–304](/Users/hong/code/pointconstellation/docs/co-adaptation-hypotheses.md:283)). Experiment 005 consequently freezes the decoder and uses a recurrent, competitive refiner initialized from FPS, optionally consuming legal decoder gradients while retaining only the final quantized coordinates in the bitstream ([co-adaptation-hypotheses.md:363–392](/Users/hong/code/pointconstellation/docs/co-adaptation-hypotheses.md:363)). Three-seed procedural results report 23.33% and 25.13% gains over FPS on validation and parameter OOD; ModelNet40 later reports 16.13% and 18.19% at \(K=8\), but no reliable aggregate advantage over the matched feature codec ([README.md:230–236](/Users/hong/code/pointconstellation/README.md:230), [README.md:278–287](/Users/hong/code/pointconstellation/README.md:278)).

**Prior art and novelty boundary.** The cited landscape covers G-PCC, Draco, V-PCC, Learned-PCGC, VoxelContext-Net, Density-Preserving PCC, JPEG Pleno, implicit representations, learned simplification, and primitive/surface codecs ([research-landscape.md:18–92](/Users/hong/code/pointconstellation/docs/research-landscape.md:18)). Its closest representation table distinguishes Irregular Convolutional AE and sparse latent diffusion by their feature channels, SampleNet and feature-preserving simplification by their non-codec objectives, and primitive codecs by explicit types/structure ([research-landscape.md:94–102](/Users/hong/code/pointconstellation/docs/research-landscape.md:94)). The inference rationale additionally cites iterative/semi-amortized inference, neural-image-codec inference optimization, DeepSDF, Deep Set Prediction, Slot Attention, DN-DETR, S-NET/SampleNet, SQ-VAE, emergent communication, and two-time-scale optimization ([co-adaptation-hypotheses.md:64–155](/Users/hong/code/pointconstellation/docs/co-adaptation-hypotheses.md:64)). Thus the defensible novelty is the **combination** of a strict same-space coordinate-only codec contract with competitive semi-amortized set inference—not coordinates, point upsampling, decoder-gradient refinement, or slot competition individually. The repository correctly calls this only a “novelty hypothesis” pending a formal review and patent search ([research-landscape.md:7–14](/Users/hong/code/pointconstellation/docs/research-landscape.md:7)).

**Claim-type verdict.** The ambition is a **compression claim**; the conceptual contribution is a **representation claim**; the best-supported current result is an **inference claim**. Compression is weakest because competitive general-purpose rate-distortion evidence is expressly unclaimed, G-PCC crosses over near 0.21 bpp, and the released learned-codec smoke is at least 35× the 50-byte rate ([README.md:270–287](/Users/hong/code/pointconstellation/README.md:270), [README.md:311–324](/Users/hong/code/pointconstellation/README.md:311)). The safest headline today is: *for a frozen shared completion decoder, recurrent input-conditioned refinement with legal decoder-gradient feedback finds fixed-\(K\), quantized coordinate messages that outperform FPS at matched low rates on procedural and ModelNet slices.* General codec competitiveness and genuinely spatial—not private-code—semantics remain hypotheses.

## Key facts

- The long-term codec is variable-\(N\), variable-\(K\), variable-output, entropy/rate controlled, and includes a charged raw pass-through mode ([adaptive-codec.md:3–36](/Users/hong/code/pointconstellation/docs/adaptive-codec.md:3)). None of those adaptive mechanisms is yet the leading empirical contribution.

- The contract mandates permutation invariance and requests architectural or measured rigid-transform equivariance ([representation-contract.md:29–40](/Users/hong/code/pointconstellation/docs/representation-contract.md:29)).

- Actual bytes, metadata, decoder size, runtime, memory, and multiple distortion-rate points—not \(N/K\) or tensor size—define a compression result ([representation-contract.md:58–70](/Users/hong/code/pointconstellation/docs/representation-contract.md:58)).

- Free-coordinate constellations, learned strict subsets, and post-hoc projected constellations are scientifically different objects. The strongest gains currently come from the first, with smaller retained gains after post-hoc projection.

- **Unverified omission—modern learned codec baselines:** reviewers would expect explicit treatment of [OctSqueeze](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content_CVPR_2020/html/Huang_OctSqueeze_Octree-Structured_Entropy_Model_for_LiDAR_Compression_CVPR_2020_paper.html), [SparsePCGC](https://arxiv.org/abs/2111.10633)/PCGCv2, and recent sparse-latent compression such as [DiffCom](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.13860). Full implementation, common-test-condition, and novelty-boundary checks were not performed here.

- **Unverified omission—point reconstruction lineage:** FoldingNet is named but not formally cited; [Achlioptas et al.](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v80/achlioptas18a), [FoldingNet](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content_cvpr_2018/html/Yang_FoldingNet_Point_Cloud_CVPR_2018_paper.html), [AtlasNet](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content_cvpr_2018/papers/Groueix_A_Papier-Mache_Approach_CVPR_2018_paper.pdf), and [PU-Net](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content_cvpr_2018/html/Yu_PU-Net_Point_Cloud_CVPR_2018_paper.html) are expected context for shared learned reconstruction from compact/sparse inputs.

- **Unverified novelty threats—coordinate-keypoint representations:** [KeypointNet](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper/2018/hash/24146db4eb48c718b84cae0a0799dcfc-Abstract.html), [Key-Grid](https://papers.nips.cc/paper_files/paper/2024/hash/582e9771ac8527cb6390e5e9444a0fee-Abstract-Conference.html), and especially [KeyPointDiffuser](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.03450) should be compared contract-by-contract. Key-Grid visibly uses encoder-side skip information; KeyPointDiffuser’s keypoints-to-shape formulation may be much closer, but its quantization, complete decoder input, rate accounting, and compression objective require full-text verification.

Quantization is correct in the forward pass: `quantize_coordinates` clamps and rounds onto the declared lattice, and `quantize_ste` returns that quantized value while applying an identity gradient estimator ([quantization.py:17](/Users/hong/code/pointconstellation/src/pointconstellation/quantization.py:17)). Bitstream accounting is sound for the fixed normalized protocol. ## Weaknesses

- The only stabilized rate is K8. Joining it to Experiment 017’s unstable K4/K16/K32 checkpoints would create a scientifically invalid RD curve.

- G-PCC decisively wins above the crossover: the old K16/K32 points are dominated, and the fixture loses increasingly badly at high rates. There is no full-curve or BD-rate superiority.

- No fair published entropy-coded learned codec has a valid overlapping point. GeoCNNv2’s closest valid diagnostic is still 111 bytes; the exact-split pilots collapsed.

- The evaluation uses a 128-cloud validation subset and 32-cloud category OOD subset, not full ModelNet40, ShapeNetCore, or MPEG/JPEG common test conditions ([scope limitation](/Users/hong/code/pointconstellation/docs/experiment-017-018-modelnet40-scale.md:242)).

- The 50-byte count assumes the normalized `[-1,1]` object domain. The 14-byte header records a domain ID but not the source mesh’s center/scale transform ([bitstream header](/Users/hong/code/pointconstellation/src/pointconstellation/bitstream.py:118)); that transform must be counted if reconstruction in original coordinates is claimed.

- The shared 405,394-byte model is excluded from bpp. Amortized over 128 clouds, the effective rate is about `12.57 bpp`; even over 672 clouds it is `2.55 bpp`. More than 35,436 clouds are needed to keep stream-plus-model amortized rate below `0.24 bpp`. External learned methods deserve the same accounting ([model-size table](/Users/hong/code/pointconstellation/docs/experiment-017-018-modelnet40-scale.md:217)).

- The refiner is approximately 17× slower to encode than the internal feature encoder. This supports an inference-quality tradeoff, not an unqualified codec-efficiency claim.

- “Learned simplification” is unsupported: free coordinates are not source subsets, while `strict_subset` is post-hoc projection rather than a trained strict-subset encoder.
